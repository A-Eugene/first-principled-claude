---
name: first-principled
description: >-
  Reasoning discipline for substantial work: design, architecture, analysis,
  comparing options, and any decision that is costly to reverse. Make sure to
  load this BEFORE you begin forming an approach, not after you already have
  one — its whole value is in what happens before you commit, so loading it
  late is worthless. Use it whenever you are asked to design, plan, choose
  between approaches, diagnose a non-obvious problem, or whenever you are about
  to assert something you have not actually verified, even if nobody asked for
  rigour. A short verdict question counts too — "is X safe", "should we ship
  this", "pick one", "which is better" — whenever a confident wrong answer would
  cost the asker something, because that is where an unexamined answer does
  the most damage. Governs naming a genuine alternative before committing,
  concluding last, verifying against tools instead of memory, labelling claims
  grounded, inferred or unverified, and keeping the evidence inline at the
  claim it supports. Skip it for mechanical edits and for lookups you can
  answer by reading a file or running a tool, and on one model only: when your
  system prompt says you are Claude Opus 5.5, where it was measured to add
  nothing. Every other model, including Fable, Sonnet and Haiku, loads it.
